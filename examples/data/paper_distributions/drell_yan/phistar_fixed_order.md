# Drell–Yan acoplanarity against fixed order

[Four comparison bins](phistar_fixed_order.csv) cover the hard part of the lepton acoplanarity spectrum, from 0.107 to 3 in φ*η. Both full shower samples and the NNLOJET reference use Born leptons with transverse momentum above 27 GeV and absolute pseudorapidity below 2.5, a dilepton mass between 66 and 116 GeV, and a collision energy of 13 TeV. Each density uses its full fiducial cross section at the corresponding scale. For positive acoplanarity the inclusive NNLO calculation contributes through NLO in Z + jet. The [ATLAS-bin comparison](atlas_phistar.csv) gives the separate comparison with data across the measured range.

The two shower predictions sum complete generator bins. The reference combines 298 common NNLOJET runs, with the fiducial normalization for each run retained in its integration error. The generator boundaries split a few of the finer fixed-order bins. Between any pair of consecutive boundaries the underlying density is taken to decrease, as observed in the fine bins throughout this region. The total weight of each fine bin and the average densities in the neighboring bins then bound the contribution on either side of a split. The reference uses the midpoint of these bounds. Their half-width is at most 0.61% over the four comparison bins and seven scales. Merging pairs of fine bins and testing the resulting bounds at the known original boundaries gave 112 successful checks.

The columns `low` and `high` give the acoplanarity range. `fixed_order`, `pythia` and `herwig` give normalized densities. The `_mc_error` column gives numerical integration errors. The `_event_error` and `_fit_input_error` columns give finite-event errors at fixed weights (including the common normalization) and the standard deviation across the 20 target-integration replicas. Fields with `_bin_low` and `_bin_high` bound the effect of splitting the fine fixed-order bins. The scale columns use the same scale order as `atlas_recoil.csv`. Coherent prediction ratios divide the fit by fixed order at each scale. The gray band in the figure spans the seven fixed-order predictions including the uncertainty from the split bins.
